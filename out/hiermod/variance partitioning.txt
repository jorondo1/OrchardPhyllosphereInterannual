Model 5 splits the total spread in log-diversity into pieces that get treated in genuinely different ways, not just given different names. Fixed effects — Management's baseline (loga[Mg]), the seasonal shift (gamma), Year (c[Yr]) — are each estimated as free, independent numbers with no shrinkage between levels: Year 2022's intercept has nothing to do with 2023's, they just happen to share a prior. Random effects — Location (b[Lo]) and Tree (tr[Tr]) — are different: each level is treated as a draw from a shared population, Normal(0, σ_loc²) or Normal(0, σ_tr²), so an unusual location or tree gets pulled back toward the group average, more so when it has less data behind it (partial pooling). That pooling is also what lets the model learn how much locations or trees typically vary (σ_loc, σ_tr) as a single population-level number, rather than trusting each individual level's raw estimate.

The residual noise — σ[cell] — is where Model 5's actual change lives, and it's a third kind of thing again: not partially pooled across cells, and not a free-standing fixed effect either, but a category-specific variance with its own independent prior per cell. Each of the four Management×Season cells gets its own σ[cell] ~ dexp(3), estimated only from that cell's own residuals, with no borrowing of strength from the other three. That's a deliberate choice, not an oversight — the modeling question was specifically "do these cells have different spread," so pooling them would undercut the very thing being tested. The tradeoff is exactly the one we mapped out earlier: partial pooling (Location, Tree) buys stability by borrowing strength across levels; category-specific variance (the four cells) buys honesty about real heterogeneity at the cost of each estimate standing on a smaller, unshared pile of data.

All three layers add up on the log scale — total_var = σ[cell]² + σ_loc² + σ_tr² — because independent variances sum. Fixed effects shift where the distribution sits; the two random-effect variances and the cell-specific residual variance jointly determine how wide it is, and that total width is what the mean/median backtransform (and, with the shift, the +1 floor) ultimately depends on.
