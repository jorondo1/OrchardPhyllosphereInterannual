Model 6 (MDLSY, "The Varietal") -- formal specification
============================================================

Paste each $$...$$ block below into a Word equation box (Insert >
Equation > switch input to "LaTeX" via the Conversions dropdown, or
Alt+= then type directly if your Word build supports linear LaTeX
input).

Convention: a proper interaction, McElreath-style (and standard HLM
cross-level-interaction notation more generally -- see e.g. the
level-1/level-2 formulation in multilevel-modelling texts): one linear
model for $\mu_i$, and inside it, the slope on Season is *itself* a
second linear model of Management, not a single number. This is
literally the `gamma <- s_conv + gap_shift*(Mg-1)` line already in
`6.1_MDLSY_model.R`, given its own equation instead of being folded in.

Naming convention: single plain letters, a through g (skipping i,
reserved for the observation index), one per fitted coefficient. sigma
is paired to its matching random-effect letter by subscript. (Model 7
picks up where this leaves off, h/j/m, for its three control covariates
-- see model7_MDLSYC_latex.txt.)

  a  = Management intercept (May baseline, index-variable form, 2 levels)
  b  = Season slope at Conventional (shared May->July shift)
  c  = Organic's EXTRA Season slope -- the interaction term itself
  d  = Location effect (random, pooled)
  e  = Tree effect (random, pooled)
  f  = Year effect (random, pooled)
  g  = Cultivar effect (fixed, unpooled -- indexed like a, not like d/e/f:
       no population SD, no shrinkage, just an independent prior per level)


1. Notation
-----------
For observation i = 1, ..., N (each row is one Tree x Season measurement):

  - H_i          raw Hill_1 diversity index
  - D_i = H_i - 1   shifted diversity, support (0, inf) -- Hill numbers
                     floor at 1, so the floor is subtracted before fitting
                     a plain (0, inf)-support LogNormal, and added back
                     only when back-transforming to a reported mean/median.
  - Mg[i] in {1, 2}     Management: 1 = Conventional, 2 = Organic
  - S_i = Mo[i]-1 in {0, 1}   Season dummy: 0 = May, 1 = July
  - Lo[i] in {1,...,4}  Location (orchard)
  - Tr[i] in {1,...,n_Tr}  Tree (study unit, nested in Location)
  - Yr[i] in {1,2,3}    Year
  - Cv[i] in {1,...,5}  Cultivar

k[i], the residual-scale index -- a plain 4-level categorical variable,
used only so $\sigma$ can differ by Management x Season cell:

  k[i] = 2*(Mg[i]-1) + Mo[i]      (Mg,Mo)=(1,1)->1, (1,2)->2, (2,1)->3, (2,2)->4


2. Likelihood
-------------
$$
D_i \sim \mathrm{LogNormal}\!\left(\mu_i,\ \sigma_{k[i]}\right)
$$


3. Linear model for $\mu$
----------------------------
$$
\mu_i = a_{\mathrm{Mg}[i]} + \gamma_{\mathrm{Mg}[i]}\, S_i
+ d_{\mathrm{Lo}[i]} + e_{\mathrm{Tr}[i]} + f_{\mathrm{Yr}[i]} + g_{\mathrm{Cv}[i]}
$$

The Season slope is itself a linear model of Management, not a single
shared number:

$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{Mg}} = b + c\,(\mathrm{Mg}-1)
$$

$b$ is the May $\to$ July slope at Conventional ($\mathrm{Mg}=1$); $c$
is how much steeper (or shallower) that slope is at Organic
($\mathrm{Mg}=2$) -- the Management $\times$ Season interaction, i.e.
the seasonal-change estimand, now a real coefficient rather than
something read off a derived contrast. ($a$ uses the index-variable
convention already used for Location/Tree/Year elsewhere in this
codebase -- a separate intercept per level rather than a baseline +
dummy offset; the two are algebraically interchangeable, index form is
just what the rest of the model already uses.)


4. Random effects
------------------
$$
d_{\mathrm{Lo}} \sim \mathrm{Normal}(0,\ \sigma_d^2), \qquad \mathrm{Lo}=1,\dots,4
$$
$$
e_{\mathrm{Tr}} \sim \mathrm{Normal}(0,\ \sigma_e^2), \qquad \mathrm{Tr}=1,\dots,n_{Tr}
$$
$$
f_{\mathrm{Yr}} \sim \mathrm{Normal}(0,\ \sigma_f^2), \qquad \mathrm{Yr}=1,2,3
$$

(Sampled via a non-centered reparameterization -- a standard-normal
auxiliary draw scaled by $\sigma$ -- purely for HMC sampling efficiency;
distributionally identical to the statement above, not a separate
modeling choice.)


5. Fixed-effect priors
-------------------------
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{\mathrm{Mg}} &\sim \mathrm{Normal}(2,2), & \mathrm{Mg}&=1,2\\
b &\sim \mathrm{Normal}(0,1)\\
c &\sim \mathrm{Normal}(0,1)\\
g_{\mathrm{Cv}} &\sim \mathrm{Normal}(0,1), & \mathrm{Cv}&=1,\dots,5
\end{aligned}
$$


6. Variance-component priors
-------------------------------
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{k} &\sim \mathrm{Exponential}(3.46), \qquad k=1,2,3,4\\
\sigma_d &\sim \mathrm{Exponential}(2.31)\\
\sigma_e &\sim \mathrm{Exponential}(2.31)\\
\sigma_f &\sim \mathrm{Exponential}(2.31)
\end{aligned}
$$

These rates are variance-budget-calibrated (R2D2M2-style): $D_i$'s
total marginal variance sums 4 components (residual + Location + Tree +
Year). Each rate is scaled by $\sqrt{4/3} \approx 1.15$ relative to the
already prior-predictive-validated Model 5 rates ($\mathrm{Exponential}(3)$
for $\sigma_k$, $\mathrm{Exponential}(2)$ for $\sigma_d/\sigma_e$), so
adding $\sigma_f$ (Year) doesn't inflate the total implied variance
beyond what was already validated.


7. Derived estimands
-----------------------
Population-level mean at Management $m$, Season $s\in\{0,1\}$
(marginalized over Location/Tree/Year), floor added back:

$$
\mathrm{E}[H \mid m,s] = 1 + \exp\!\left(a_m + \gamma_m s + \tfrac{1}{2}V_{k(m,s)}\right),
\qquad V_k = \sigma_k^2 + \sigma_d^2 + \sigma_e^2 + \sigma_f^2
$$

Headline contrasts (the "+1" floor cancels in every difference below):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{May gap} &= \mathrm{E}[H\mid 2,0] - \mathrm{E}[H\mid 1,0]\\
\text{July gap} &= \mathrm{E}[H\mid 2,1] - \mathrm{E}[H\mid 1,1]\\
\text{Seasonal change} &= \text{July gap} - \text{May gap}
\end{aligned}
$$

The seasonal-change estimand is where $c$ actually shows up: at
$s=0$ both gaps depend only on $a_1,a_2$ (May, no slope yet); at $s=1$
the July gap picks up $b$ (shared) and $c$ (Organic-only) on top of that
-- so "seasonal change" isolates $c$ up to the nonlinearity introduced
by $\exp(\cdot)$ and the $\tfrac{1}{2}V$ term. This is why $c$
(`gap_shift`) is the interaction estimand even though it's never quoted
as a raw posterior on its own -- every reported contrast is a
transformation of it.
